Features

Built for the table, not your pocket

Everything is sized to be read and tapped by six people sitting around a phone lying flat on the board.

One tap passes the turn

Only the active player's wedge responds, so nobody ends someone else's turn by leaning on the screen. Two-player games split into facing halves instead.

Up to 6 players

Names, ten meeple colours, drag-to-reorder seats, and a random first player.

A turn limit with teeth

10 seconds to 5 minutes. Go over and your clock turns red, double-beeps and buzzes.

Results worth arguing about

A crown for the fastest player, and a summary ready for the group chat.

Forgiving in the moment

Pause, resume, and a one-level undo for the inevitable mis-tap.

Game history that sticks around

Every finished game is saved on your phone — name, date, rounds and each player's time — and browsable later from the Statistics tab, alongside a whole-game clock and round counter while you play.

Quick Timer

Start a 1–6 player game on your saved defaults in two taps.

Made for dim game nights

A dark theme, hand-drawn meeples, and keep-screen-awake.

Two modes

Count up, or count down

Pick when you set the game up. The per-turn limit works in both.

Mode one

Stopwatch

Every player's clock simply counts up. No limits, no pressure — just a scoreboard of who thinks the longest.

  • Nobody can run out of time
  • Best for co-ops and long euros
  • Settles "who is the slow one?" for good
Mode two

Countdown

Each player gets a time bank — 30 minutes by default, anywhere from 5 to 180 — that drains while they think.

  • Running out is soft: the clock goes negative in red and alarms once
  • Nobody is eliminated — the table decides what it means
  • Results show how much time each player had left
How it works

Three steps to a timed game

  1. Set the table

    Add players, name the game, and choose Stopwatch or Countdown and a turn limit.

  2. Phone in the middle

    Lay it flat on the board. Everyone's wedge points at them; the active one glows.

  3. Tap and play

    Finish your turn, tap your own wedge, and the clock passes clockwise.

Oz Soffy

iOS developer · Designer · Board gamer

Meeple Clock is designed, built, drawn and shipped by Oz Soffy. Its timer engine runs on date maths rather than accumulated ticks, so the clocks stay honest even over a four-hour game of Twilight Imperium.

The whole project is open source — code, design history and marketing assets. Bug reports and feature requests are welcome on GitHub.

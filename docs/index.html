Meeple Clock

A chess clock for the whole table.

Put your iPhone flat in the middle of the board. Every player gets their own glowing wedge of the screen — tap yours to pass the turn, and settle once and for all who is taking forever.

Built for the table, not for your pocket

Everything is sized to be read and tapped by six people sitting around a phone lying flat on the board.

🎲

Up to 6 players

Each gets a name and one of ten gradient meeple colours. Drag the seats into the order you are actually sitting in, and pick who starts — or let the app roll for it.

⏱️

One tap to pass the turn

Only the active player's wedge is tappable, so nobody can end someone else's turn by leaning on the screen. Two-player games split into two facing halves instead.

🚨

A per-turn limit with teeth

Set anything from 10 seconds to 5 minutes. Go over and your clock turns red, double-beeps and buzzes — gentle public shaming, exactly as intended.

👑

Results worth arguing about

A crown for the fastest player, every total side by side, and a plain-text summary you can drop straight into the group chat.

📊

Game history that sticks around

Every finished game is saved on your phone — name, date, rounds and per-player times — and browsable later from the Statistics tab.

↩️

Forgiving in the moment

Pause and resume, a one-level undo for the inevitable mis-tap, a whole-game clock and a round counter running the entire time.

⚡

Quick Timer

Already know the drill? Start a 1–6 player game on your saved defaults in two taps, no setup screen in the way.

Made for dim game nights

A fixed dark, silver-accented theme, hand-drawn meeple pawns, and an option to keep the screen awake so the phone never sleeps mid-turn.

Two ways to run the clock

Pick the mode when you set the game up. The per-turn limit works in both.

Mode one

Stopwatch

Every player's clock simply counts up. No limits, no pressure — you are just keeping score of who thinks the longest.

  • Nobody can run out of time
  • Best for co-ops and long euros
  • Answers "who is the AP player?" for good

Mode two

Countdown

Each player gets a fixed time bank — 30 minutes by default, anywhere from 5 to 180 — that drains while they think.

  • Running out is soft: the clock goes negative in red and alarms once
  • Nobody is eliminated, no turn is auto-passed — the table decides what it means
  • Results show how much time each player had left

Three taps to a timed game

1

Set the table

Add your players, name the game, choose Stopwatch or Countdown and a per-turn limit.

2

Phone in the middle

Lay it flat on the board. Everyone's wedge points at them; the active one glows and pulses.

3

Tap and play

Finish your turn, tap your own wedge, and the clock passes clockwise to the next player.

About the app

Meeple Clock started as a small annoyance at a game night — one player who always took three times as long as everyone else, and no way to prove it. It is now a native iPhone app, hand-built in SwiftUI, with a fixed dark theme and a set of meeple pawns drawn from scratch.

Your game night stays yours

Player names, game names and your entire saved history live on your device and are never transmitted anywhere. The app reports anonymous usage statistics — which screens get opened, how many players a game had — and none of what you type is in them. No accounts, no tracking, no advertising identifier.

Oz Soffy

iOS developer · Designer · Board gamer

Meeple Clock is designed, built, drawn and shipped by Oz Soffy. It is written in pure SwiftUI — no UIKit view controllers anywhere — with a drift-free timer engine built on date maths rather than accumulated ticks, so the clocks stay honest even over a four-hour game of Twilight Imperium.

The whole project is open source: the code, the design history, and the marketing assets are all on GitHub. Bug reports and feature requests are welcome there.
